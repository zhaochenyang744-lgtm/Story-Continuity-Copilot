# Chapter 1 The Face Inspection

At 12:05 on 21 June, the rectangular working face of stamping die R5 had a total area of three hundred square millimeters. Its width was twenty millimeters. Brenna Keel copied the inspection result beside a sketch of the whole face, not merely its border.

# Chapter 2 The Die Height

At 12:05 on 21 June, the same flat working face of stamping die R5 was exactly fifteen millimeters high, measured perpendicular to its width. An assistant checked the height independently and left the die on the inspection cloth.

# Chapter 3 The Drying Batch

At 13:30 on 21 June, drying batch T7 was still being processed and its drying cycle had not finished. The sheets were damp, and the operator kept the cycle running. The earlier pulp-mixing stage had already ended, but it was a separate stage.

# Chapter 4 The Rag Sorting Table

At 09:15 on 21 June, Idris Foyle separated the red cotton scraps from the plain ones on the sorting table. He put the red pieces in a wicker tray and brushed loose threads off the scale before weighing the next delivery.

# Chapter 5 Wet Pulp on the Scale

Pulp lot Umbel weighed eighty kilograms while wet. That measurement included its retained water, and no dry-weight measurement or water fraction was recorded. The delivery note names the fibers but supplies no conversion from wet mass to dry mass.

# Chapter 6 The Earlier Layout

An earlier floor plan places press Cobalt beside the west loading door. The building was rearranged after that plan was drawn, but the relocation list is missing. The remaining notes do not identify which machines moved or stayed in place.

# Chapter 7 Paper Beside the Window

At 16:20 on 21 June, Brenna Keel clipped three unprinted sheets to a drying line beside the narrow window. She placed a clean tray beneath them to catch drips, then rolled the unused line back onto its wooden holder.

# Chapter 8 The Evening Bundle

Idris tied the unused scraps into a loose bundle and left the inspection cloth on the workbench. He kept the old floor plan with the unresolved relocation notes, rather than treating it as a current map. The loading door rattled gently in the evening wind.
